The operating problem

Business conversations do not begin and end with a chatbot response. They become orders, bookings, follow-ups, support cases, staff interventions, contact updates, and records that other systems need to understand.

Hader brings those activities into one operating model while preserving a clear boundary between automated actions and human control.

Technical implementation

Hader is an event-driven, multi-tenant operating system for business conversations. The central design problem is not generating a reply; it is preserving tenant ownership, conversation state, human control, and the integrity of downstream business actions while messages and calls arrive through different channels.

Architecture and request flow

  1. 01 · IngressValidate and attribute the event

    A channel webhook, connector event, or voice bridge request is authenticated and resolved to the organization and channel that own it.

  2. 02 · PersistenceRecord state before automation

    Contacts, threads, messages, delivery state, and relevant unhandled fields are persisted so a failed AI or downstream call does not erase the source event.

  3. 03 · Policy gateDecide whether automation may act

    Organization features, consent, channel configuration, bot state, human-pause state, and conversation context are evaluated before a reply path is opened.

  4. 04 · DecisionGround the response and proposed action

    Approved business information and structured records provide context. Orders, bookings, payment links, contact changes, and escalation are treated as typed operations rather than unbounded model text.

  5. 05 · DeliverySend through the owning channel

    The appropriate adapter delivers the response or action, records provenance and status, and moves retryable or scheduled work to background processing.

  6. 06 · Human and operationsKeep one operational history

    Staff work from the same durable thread, can pause or resume automation explicitly, and can inspect operational and audit state without a parallel conversation history.

System invariants

  • Tenant ownership is resolved first

    Business configuration and records are organization-scoped, with tenant-isolation and row-level-access checks covering the persistence boundary.

  • Human control overrides automation

    A paused or handed-off thread cannot receive an automated reply until a defined resume path permits it.

  • Business actions are validated

    Model output cannot directly become an order, booking, payment request, or contact mutation without passing the relevant domain rules.

  • Imported context keeps provenance

    Contact synchronization, captured history, and mirrored records retain source information so operators can distinguish imported data from native Hader activity.

  • Consent gates historical capture

    Connecting a channel does not silently authorize every historical-data operation; the relevant capture path is explicitly gated.

  • Voice remains a channel adapter

    Telephony resolves organization context and invokes Hader operations; it does not create a second source of truth for orders, bookings, or customer history.

Key engineering decisions and tradeoffs

Persist the inbound event before generating a reply
Why: preserves a recoverable conversation record when model, channel, or downstream services fail. Tradeoff: adds database work to the latency-sensitive path.
Move retryable and scheduled work out of the request path
Why: webhook delivery, broadcasts, reminders, exports, consistency checks, and cleanup have different timing and retry requirements. Tradeoff: introduces eventual consistency and requires idempotent workers and operational visibility.
Represent handoff as durable state, not prompt wording
Why: a system rule can suppress automation reliably across workers and channels. Tradeoff: pause expiry, manual resume, and stale-state recovery become explicit lifecycle problems.
Keep telephony behind a separate bridge
Why: isolates call transport and real-time audio concerns from the core business model. Tradeoff: creates a distributed failure boundary that needs call lifecycle reconciliation and reaping.
Federate Alinia instead of sharing its database
Why: preserves product ownership and access boundaries while enabling approved identity and listing flows. Tradeoff: mirrored data is eventually consistent and needs provisioning, reset, and deprovision paths.
Support WhatsApp Coexistence instead of replacing the handset workflow
Why: lets staff retain the familiar business-phone workflow. Tradeoff: handset replies, platform replies, history capture, and attribution must be reconciled explicitly.

Failure handling and verification

  • Boundary tests

    Tenant-isolation, row-level-access drift, webhook signatures, multi-number routing, and provenance-access tests exercise the highest-risk ownership boundaries.

  • Conversation safety tests

    Handoff detection, bot suppression, Coexistence capture, contact-sync planning, order confirmation, booking text, and voice-order matching are tested as domain behavior.

  • Operational reconciliation

    Inbox-consistency work, stale-pause cleanup, call reaping, delivery retries, health probes, and data migrations address failures that only appear across time or services.

  • Deployment discipline

    CI checks, production health verification, documented rollout policy, and a formal handover reduce dependence on one engineer’s local knowledge.

  • Known limit

    The page does not claim a universal latency, accuracy, uptime, compliance certification, or autonomous-resolution rate; those require separately defined production evidence.

Voice as a Hader capability

Voice AI is implemented inside Hader rather than presented as a separate product. The capability connects live telephone calls with Hader’s conversation, order, booking, payment, transfer, and call-record workflows.

The voice capability is deployed and in active client use. It remains a supporting capability within the broader Hader platform rather than a standalone product.
