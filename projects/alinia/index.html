From search feature to operating system

Property discovery sits inside a larger workflow: listings must be managed, agencies and users need appropriate access, inquiries move across web and messaging channels, and conversations eventually require a person or an operational follow-up.

Alinia was engineered around that full path instead of treating AI search as an isolated interface.

My contribution

I worked with Tayseer Laz and the Aligned Tech team across the system’s architecture and delivery. My contribution spanned AI services, backend and application workflows, agency and listing operations, authentication and access boundaries, CRM and reporting features, performance work, deployment, and integration with Hader.

The work also connected web discovery with WhatsApp and managed human handoff, allowing customer conversations to move into operational workflows instead of remaining isolated chat sessions.

Technical implementation

Alinia separates its public discovery experience, application services, AI services, and messaging work while keeping listings and organizational permissions as the authoritative operating context.

  • Grounded property discoveryAI-assisted requests are converted into structured search criteria and applied to listing data rather than allowing a model to invent available properties.
  • Listing lifecycleCreation, review, approval, publication, reassignment, sale, rental, and archive states feed both the public experience and internal operations.
  • Role and agency boundariesPublic users, agents, agency administrators, and platform administrators operate through distinct authentication and authorization paths.
  • Messaging and CRMWeb inquiries, customer records, assignments, broadcasts, WhatsApp activity, and staff takeover connect discovery to follow-up work.
  • Hader federationControlled approval, identity federation, provisioning, and queued listing synchronization connect Alinia to Hader without merging the two products.
  • Performance and deliveryPaginated and parallelized listing reads, cache invalidation on state changes, automated tests, deployment workflows, and production fixes support the live application.

This is a systems-level explanation of employer-owned work. It omits private code, listing and customer data, administrative screens, secrets, and infrastructure.
