Internal operations · Employer-owned

Bridge

One internal-tool pattern, adapted into two separate systems for the way Aligned Tech and Achour Holding manage projects, communication, and operational visibility.

The shared idea

Operational work is often fragmented across messages, documents, task systems, and individual follow-ups. Bridge creates a controlled place where teams can see what is being delivered, what has changed, and what needs attention.

I worked on two employer-specific implementations. They share a product philosophy and several operating patterns, but they are separate systems owned by their respective organizations.

Shared system pattern

  • Project visibilityA consolidated view of active work, status, milestones, and progress.
  • Role-scoped accessPeople see the projects and operational information appropriate to their role or organization.
  • Structured communicationUpdates, requests, remarks, and activity remain attached to the work they concern.
  • Operational recordsImportant changes and actions are recorded so delivery does not depend on memory or scattered chat threads.

Two implementations

  • Aligned Tech · InternalSLA Bridge

    A service-delivery and client-operations system connecting company records, projects, tickets, visits, contracts, performance, reporting, and related internal workflows.

  • Achour Holding · InternalAchour Holding Bridge

    A collaboration system for the technical department and subsidiaries, covering project progress, milestones, remarks, controlled content delivery, and organization-specific access.

Applied, not copied

The useful part of Bridge is not one fixed interface. It is the operating model: make ownership visible, keep communication beside the work, limit access deliberately, and integrate with the tools an organization already uses.

At Aligned Tech, that model expanded around service delivery and client operations. At Achour Holding, it was adapted to the relationship between a central technical department and multiple subsidiaries. Each implementation follows its organization’s structure instead of forcing both companies into the same workflow.

Publication boundary

Both implementations are internal and employer-owned. This case study describes the product thinking and my work at a high level; it does not publish source code, private dashboards, customer or subsidiary data, credentials, integrations, or infrastructure details.
