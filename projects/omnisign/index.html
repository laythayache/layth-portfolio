OmniSign

Accessibility · Computer vision · Team project

Translation begins with understanding the signs that already exist.

OmniSign is an AI-powered platform for real-time Lebanese and American Sign Language recognition. We built the data, models, and application together because usable Lebanese Sign Language datasets were not available to us.

Why it started

A communication problem, not a model demo.

Co-founder Tayseer Laz traces the project’s origin to a clinic encounter during the COVID period, when a deaf man struggled to share a simple joke. The moment exposed how ordinary conversations can become inaccessible when an interpreter is unavailable.

Our first assumption was that sign-language data could be sourced and adapted. Instead, we found significant variation between regions—including Beirut, the Beqaa, and the South—and even between schools in the same area. Roughly three months of travel and collection followed, making the data program the foundation of the product.

Recognition scope

Several modes, one experience.

  • Individual lettersRecognition for isolated alphabet signs and spelling flows.
  • Everyday and clinical vocabularyAn early set of common expressions, with selected medical terms prioritized for higher-stakes conversations.
  • Continuous signingTemporal recognition across sequences instead of relying only on individual static frames.
  • LSL and ASLLebanese Sign Language was the central data challenge, with American Sign Language included in the product.
  • Automatic model selectionThe multimodel pipeline routes input without asking the user to manually choose a recognition mode first.
  • Text and speech outputRecognized signs can be surfaced as readable text and spoken output for the other participant.

Engineering approach

Live input, specialized models, accessible output.

01Capture

Live camera input records the signer’s movement.

02Represent

Computer vision extracts spatial and temporal landmarks.

03Recognize

Specialized models handle letters, vocabulary, and continuous sequences.

04Route

The multimodel layer chooses a suitable recognition path.

05Translate

The application presents the result as text and speech.

The public collaborator case study reports a 98% internal evaluation result under controlled conditions. It also describes sub-100 ms recognition as a target and an LSTM-based sequential model. These figures are useful engineering context, not universal performance guarantees: lighting, camera angle, signer, vocabulary, and signing speed all affect results.

The product direction also included an interactive learning experience, screen-reader and high-contrast support, haptic feedback, and on-device inference where possible. Edge fallback and privacy-preserving future training were roadmap directions rather than claims of universal offline operation.

Project notes

Useful context

Is OmniSign a universal sign-language translator?

No. It supports defined Lebanese and American Sign Language recognition paths and a bounded vocabulary. Sign languages are not universal, and Lebanese usage varies by region and teaching context.

Who was the product designed for?

The intended users include deaf and hard-of-hearing people, families, educators, and healthcare workers—especially when an interpreter is not readily available.

Does the reported accuracy apply everywhere?

No. The 98% figure comes from an internal controlled evaluation reported in the collaborator case study. Real-world results vary with capture conditions, signer variation, signing speed, and vocabulary.

Is the project finished?

The award-winning FYP established the working system. The published product direction continues toward more clinical vocabulary, broader validation, and newer sequence-model research.

What this case study does not claim

OmniSign is presented as an accessibility research and engineering platform, not a replacement for human interpreters or proof that Lebanese Sign Language has been standardized. The award was earned by the team, and performance numbers remain bounded by their evaluation conditions.
