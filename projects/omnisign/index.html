OmniSign

A sign-language recognition system built around the difficult part first: collecting, cleaning, modeling, and integrating data that did not already exist in a usable form.

Watch the full demoRHU award announcementRead the Medium article

My role

I led the AI and data work: collection, processing, cleaning, model training, evaluation workflows, and integration of the recognition models into the application.

The application combined several recognition modes and selected between them automatically—closer to language auto-detection than requiring the user to choose a model manually.

What the system recognized

  • Individual lettersRecognition for isolated alphabet signs.
  • Selected clinical vocabularyWords chosen for communication contexts where misunderstanding can carry greater consequences.
  • Continuous signingTemporal recognition across sequences rather than isolated static frames alone.
  • LSL and ASLLebanese Sign Language was the central data challenge, with American Sign Language support also explored.

Dataset work

Lebanese variations were collected through 21 sign-language schools and teaching contexts. The counts below refer to different stages of the pipeline rather than interchangeable totals.

  • 40,000Individual sign samples collected.
  • 50,000Samples after augmentation.
  • 80,000Landmark records extracted for model workflows.
  • 21 schoolsVariation represented across Lebanese teaching contexts.

Current limits

OmniSign is presented as a research and engineering prototype, not a universal interpreter or a claim that Lebanese Sign Language has been fully standardized. Recognition quality depends on the signer, capture conditions, vocabulary, and model path.
