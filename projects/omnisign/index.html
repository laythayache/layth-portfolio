OmniSign

Accessibility · Computer vision · Team project

Translation begins with understanding the signs that already exist.

OmniSign is an AI-powered platform for real-time Lebanese and American Sign Language recognition. We built the data, models, and application together because usable Lebanese Sign Language datasets were not available to us.

Why it started

It started with a conversation that couldn’t happen.

OmniSign started with something Tayseer experienced at a clinic during COVID. A deaf man was trying to share a simple joke, but without an interpreter, even that became difficult. That stayed with us. It was such an ordinary moment, and it shouldn’t have been so hard.

At first, we thought we’d find an existing sign-language dataset and adapt it. But once we started collecting, we saw how much signs varied between Beirut, the Beqaa, and the South—even between schools in the same area. We spent roughly three months traveling and collecting examples. That work became the foundation of OmniSign.

Recognition scope

Several modes, one experience.

  • Individual lettersRecognition for isolated alphabet signs and spelling flows.
  • Everyday and clinical vocabularyAn early set of common expressions, with selected medical terms prioritized for higher-stakes conversations.
  • Continuous signingTemporal recognition across sequences instead of relying only on individual static frames.
  • LSL and ASLLebanese Sign Language was the central data challenge, with American Sign Language included in the product.
  • Automatic model selectionThe multimodel pipeline routes input without asking the user to manually choose a recognition mode first.
  • Text and speech outputRecognized signs can be surfaced as readable text and spoken output for the other participant.

Engineering approach

Live input, specialized models, accessible output.

01Capture

Live camera input records the signer’s movement.

02Represent

Computer vision extracts spatial and temporal landmarks.

03Recognize

Specialized models handle letters, vocabulary, and continuous sequences.

04Route

The multimodel layer chooses a suitable recognition path.

05Translate

The application presents the result as text and speech.

Tayseer has published a 98% result from one of our controlled internal evaluations, along with our sub-100 ms recognition target and the use of an LSTM-based sequential model. I treat those numbers as engineering context, not as a promise that the system performs the same way everywhere. Lighting, camera angle, signer, vocabulary, and signing speed all affect the result.

We also explored an interactive learning experience, screen-reader and high-contrast support, haptic feedback, and on-device inference where possible. Edge fallback and privacy-preserving training were directions we wanted to pursue, not claims that every part of OmniSign already worked offline.

Project notes

Useful context

Is OmniSign a universal sign-language translator?

No. We built defined Lebanese and American Sign Language recognition paths around a bounded vocabulary. Sign languages are not universal, and we saw firsthand how Lebanese usage varies by region and teaching context.

Who did you design it for?

We designed OmniSign for deaf and hard-of-hearing people, families, educators, and healthcare workers—especially in moments when an interpreter is not readily available.

Does the reported accuracy apply everywhere?

No. The 98% figure comes from one of our controlled internal evaluations. Real-world results change with the capture conditions, signer, signing speed, and vocabulary.

Is the project finished?

We built a working system for the award-winning FYP, but I do not see the problem as finished. The next steps are more clinical vocabulary, broader validation, and newer sequence-model research.

What this case study does not claim

I present OmniSign as an accessibility research and engineering platform, not as a replacement for human interpreters or proof that Lebanese Sign Language has been standardized. We earned the award as a team, and I keep every performance number tied to the conditions in which we measured it.
