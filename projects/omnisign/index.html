Why it started

It started with a joke nobody laughed at.

During COVID, I met a deaf man at a clinic. He wasn’t struggling to be understood—the staff eventually figured out what he needed through context and gestures.

What I noticed was different. He was trying to tell a joke. You could see it in his face, his timing, and the way he paused for a reaction that never came. Nobody understood him enough to laugh with him.

He had every right to share that moment with the people around him—easier than that. That was when OmniSign became more than an idea.

Our university gave us the option to choose from faculty-approved projects. We chose to find our own challenge instead. It felt overwhelming. But it felt right.

Recognition scope

Several modes, one experience.

  • Individual lettersRecognition for isolated alphabet signs and spelling flows.
  • Everyday vocabularyMore than 30 daily words and expressions, with clinical vocabulary planned as the next priority.
  • Sequence recognitionAn LSTM-based path recognizes movement across a sequence instead of treating every frame in isolation.
  • LSL and basic ASLLebanese Sign Language is our primary focus, supported by the dataset we collected ourselves.
  • Text and speech outputRecognized signs can be presented as readable text and spoken output for the other person.
  • Learning and accessibilityWe also explored guided lessons, screen-reader support, high contrast, and haptic feedback.

Built from the ground up

A language with no map.

We entered this project assuming data existed. It didn’t. We assumed sign language was universal. It wasn’t. Beqaa was different from the South, the South was different from Beirut, and even schools within Beirut signed differently.

Engineering approach

Live input, specialized models, accessible output.

01Capture

Live camera input records the signer’s movement.

02Represent

Computer vision extracts spatial and temporal landmarks.

03Recognize

Specialized models handle letters, vocabulary, and continuous sequences.

04Route

The multimodel layer chooses a suitable recognition path.

05Translate

The application presents the result as text and speech.

We used MediaPipe to extract landmark features and trained an LSTM network for sequence recognition. In our controlled internal evaluation, we reached 98% accuracy. We were surprised too. Real-world results still change with lighting, camera quality, the signer, signing speed, and vocabulary.

Our pipeline was designed for real-time translation, with text and speech output and a sub-100 ms latency target. We also explored on-device inference, edge fallback, and privacy-preserving training as the system moved beyond the first prototype.

Project notes

Useful context

Which sign languages do you support?

We currently support Lebanese Sign Language and basic American Sign Language. LSL is our primary focus, and we built its project dataset from scratch with samples from Beqaa, South Lebanon, and Beirut.

Is this real-time translation or batch processing?

Real-time. Our computer-vision pipeline processes video frames as they arrive. You sign, and it translates.

How accurate is it?

We reached 98% in our controlled internal evaluations. Real-world results vary with lighting, camera quality, the signer, signing speed, and vocabulary.

How did you collect and label the data?

We collected it in-house through events, community partnerships, and Deaf organizations. We recorded volunteers signing, extracted landmark features with MediaPipe, and labeled the data manually with community guidance.

What model architecture did you use?

We used an LSTM network for sequence recognition. Rami and I also explored more scalable approaches, including transformers, for the next iteration.

Who is this for?

Everyone. If you’re deaf, OmniSign helps you communicate. If you want to speak with a deaf person, it helps bridge that gap. Our next focus is clinical vocabulary, where miscommunication carries the highest cost.

What this case study does not claim

I present OmniSign as an accessibility research and engineering platform, not as a replacement for human interpreters or proof that Lebanese Sign Language has been standardized. We earned the award as a team, and I keep every performance number tied to the conditions in which we measured it.
