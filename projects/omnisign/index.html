Why it started

It started with a joke nobody laughed at.

During COVID, I met a deaf man at a clinic. He wasn’t struggling to be understood—the staff eventually figured out what he needed through context and gestures.

What I noticed was different. He was trying to tell a joke. You could see it in his face, his timing, and the way he paused for a reaction that never came. Nobody understood him enough to laugh with him.

He had every right to share that moment with the people around him—easier than that. That was when OmniSign became more than an idea.

Our university gave us the option to choose from faculty-approved projects. We chose to find our own challenge instead. It felt overwhelming. But it felt right.

Recognition scope

Several modes, one experience.

  • Individual lettersRecognition for isolated alphabet signs and spelling flows.
  • Everyday vocabularyMore than 30 daily words and expressions, plus a small experimental set of clinical terms.
  • Bounded sequence recognitionAn LSTM-based path recognizes movement within defined temporal sequences; it is not unrestricted continuous translation.
  • LSL and basic ASLLebanese Sign Language is our primary focus, supported by the dataset we collected ourselves.
  • Text and speech outputRecognized signs can be presented as readable text and spoken output for the other person.
  • Learning and accessibilityWe also explored guided lessons, screen-reader support, high contrast, and haptic feedback.

Built from the ground up

A language with no map.

We entered this project assuming data existed. It didn’t. We assumed sign language was universal. It wasn’t. Beqaa differed from the South, the South differed from Beirut, and variations appeared across 21 schools or teaching traditions. Those are sources of signing variation, not formal school partnerships.

Technical implementation

Technical implementation: live input, specialized models, accessible output.

01Capture

Live camera input records the signer’s movement.

02Represent

Computer vision extracts spatial and temporal landmarks.

03Recognize

Specialized models handle letters, vocabulary, and bounded sequences.

04Route

The multimodel layer chooses a suitable recognition path.

05Translate

The application presents the result as text and speech.

We used MediaPipe to extract landmark features and trained an LSTM network for recognition within bounded temporal sequences. A narrow controlled internal evaluation reached 98%; that result does not represent unrestricted translation or general real-world accuracy. Results still change with lighting, camera quality, the signer, signing speed, and vocabulary.

The application supports live camera input with text and speech output inside the current R&D scope. Broader vocabularies, unconstrained signing, and performance across unfamiliar users and environments remain active research problems.

Project notes

Useful context

Which sign languages do you support?

We currently support Lebanese Sign Language and basic American Sign Language. LSL is our primary focus, and we built its project dataset from scratch with samples from Beqaa, South Lebanon, and Beirut.

Is this unrestricted real-time translation?

No. The application processes live camera input, but the current models recognize isolated signs and bounded temporal sequences within the project vocabulary. Unrestricted continuous translation remains a research goal.

How accurate is it?

A narrow controlled internal evaluation reached 98%. That figure applies only to its defined evaluation conditions; it is not a claim of general accuracy across unfamiliar signers, environments, or unrestricted signing.

How did you collect and label the data?

We collected 40,000+ samples before augmentation through community events, host organizations, families, and volunteers. We extracted landmark features with MediaPipe, labeled the data with community guidance, and expanded the training set to 50,000+ samples through augmentation.

What model architecture did you use?

We used an LSTM network for recognition within bounded temporal sequences. The team has also explored more scalable approaches, including transformers, for later research.

Who is this for?

OmniSign explores tools that can support communication between Deaf and hearing people. It is not presented as a replacement for human interpreters. A small clinical vocabulary is experimental, with broader clinical coverage planned.

What this case study does not claim

I present OmniSign as an accessibility research and engineering platform, not as a replacement for human interpreters or proof that Lebanese Sign Language has been standardized. We earned the award as a team, and I keep every performance number tied to the conditions in which we measured it.
