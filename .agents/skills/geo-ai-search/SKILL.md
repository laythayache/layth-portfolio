# GEO and AI Search

Improve evidence quality and retrievability for generated answers while
keeping platform access, citations, representation, referrals, and conversions
as separate measurable outcomes.

## Identify the product and retrieval path

1. Name the actual AI product being evaluated. Do not infer behavior from the
   model family alone.
2. Check whether it uses public search, a partner index, user-triggered
   browsing, supplied documents, training data, or no retrieval for the task.
3. Browse current official platform documentation before naming crawlers or
   recommending access controls. Distinguish search discovery, user-triggered
   fetching, and model-training controls.
4. Treat crawler access as eligibility only. A bot visit is not evidence of
   indexing, selection, citation, or correct synthesis.

## Evaluate answerability

For each important audience question, identify one best authoritative page and
check that it provides:

- a direct, concise answer in visible text;
- precise names, aliases, roles, dates, status, and relationships;
- explicit personal contribution and honest team attribution;
- definitions and measurement conditions for material metrics;
- relevant architecture, constraints, decisions, outcomes, and limitations;
- supporting internal links and stable canonical URLs;
- authorship, update context, and public corroboration where available.

Prefer original evidence and useful specificity. Do not optimize for arbitrary
sentence lengths, content chunks, keyword repetition, or supposed secret AI
markup. Use machine-readable summaries only when they remain consistent with
the canonical human-readable pages.

## Make scoped improvements

- Correct contradictions before adding new material.
- Strengthen weak evidence on the canonical page instead of cloning thin
  summaries across routes.
- Keep structured data, metadata, `llms.txt`, feeds, repositories, and profile
  descriptions aligned with visible portfolio facts.
- Preserve crawler-training choices. Search inclusion and training permission
  are separate decisions.
- Do not add an unsupported claim merely because it would answer a desirable
  query.

## Measure rather than promise

Maintain a repeatable prompt set derived from demonstrated work. Include brand,
role, capability, project, and problem-oriented questions. For every
observation record:

- product and surface;
- date, language, location or account context when known;
- exact prompt;
- whether the entity appeared;
- whether a portfolio URL was cited;
- cited URL and supporting passage;
- factual accuracy and material omissions;
- the sample denominator.

Compare like-for-like samples before and after meaningful changes. One answer
is anecdotal and must not be generalized to all users or sessions.

## Guardrails

- Never promise an AI Overview, AI Mode answer, citation, recommendation, or
  knowledge panel.
- Never fabricate third-party mentions, reviews, customers, coverage, or
  notability.
- Do not claim that `llms.txt`, schema, crawler allowlisting, or submission is
  required by every platform or guarantees selection.
- Clearly label inferences about retrieval behavior when official sources do
  not establish it.

## Completion standard

Report the evaluated products, accessible evidence, changes made, untested
surfaces, observed citation denominator, factual errors, and the next repeated
measurement window.
