# Online Presence

Make the public record consistently establish who Layth Ayache is, what he has
demonstrably built, what he personally owned, and how an appropriate recruiter,
client, collaborator, or organization can contact him.

## Maintain a claim ledger

Before changing consequential copy, reconcile the claim across the current
repository, relevant Git history, the live site, project documentation, and
available public profiles. Record:

- the claim and canonical wording;
- affected pages and external surfaces;
- source and date;
- `verified`, `reasonable inference`, `unsupported`, or `needs confirmation`;
- what may be published and what must remain confidential;
- personal contribution, team contribution, employer ownership, and current
  project status;
- metric definition, evaluation context, and supporting artifact when a number
  is used.

Do not silently choose between contradictions. Prefer primary records and
user-confirmed facts, and preserve a traceable limitation when certainty is
not available.

## Evaluate the public identity

Check whether important surfaces consistently communicate:

- primary identity: an AI engineer who turns data and models into usable
  systems and owns the engineering around them;
- primary strengths: end-to-end AI systems and integration, applied computer
  vision and data engineering, and conversational AI or workflow automation
  with human handoff;
- technical leadership through specific decisions and ownership rather than
  unsupported adjectives;
- current role, organization spelling, location, project names and aliases,
  dates, team attribution, and public contact paths;
- demonstrated experience separately from future interests and production
  systems separately from prototypes, research, or recommendations.

Keep the framing applicable to businesses, healthcare, public institutions,
and other organizations when the evidence supports it.

## Strengthen evidence and conversion

- Make each project answer the problem, audience, personal role, team role,
  constraints, architecture, data lifecycle, decisions, integrations, testing,
  deployment status, operational ownership, outcomes, limitations, and current
  status that can safely be published.
- Remove vague technology lists when they do not demonstrate depth.
- Connect articles, projects, employers, profiles, and public deployments with
  descriptive links and consistent names.
- Use one clear contact path and state the kinds of problems for which contact
  is appropriate.
- Seek legitimate corroboration from existing organizations, collaborators,
  publications, repositories, or deployed products. Do not manufacture
  notability or create deceptive profiles.

## Prioritize

1. Identity, attribution, confidentiality, and factual errors.
2. Unsupported or undefined claims that create credibility risk.
3. Missing ownership, architecture, operational, or limitation evidence.
4. Cross-surface entity consistency and discovery.
5. Supporting content and measured conversion improvements.

## Guardrails

- Do not invent or inflate seniority, scale, performance, compliance,
  production maturity, adoption, awards, partnerships, or client outcomes.
- Do not expose source code, private dashboards, customer data, infrastructure,
  or employer-confidential implementation details.
- Do not propose Wikipedia, Wikidata, business listings, media coverage, or
  reviews unless the person or organization genuinely satisfies the platform's
  rules and the information is independently supportable.
- Do not mistake an "About this result" panel for a Google knowledge panel.

## Completion standard

Report corrected claims, their evidence, affected surfaces, publishability,
remaining contradictions, conversion impact, and the next external surface or
measurement to verify.
